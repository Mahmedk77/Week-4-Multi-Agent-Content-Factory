Solid-State Batteries: What Changes for Electric Cars

Published Oct 24, 20263 sources143 words

Solid-state batteries replace the liquid electrolyte in today's lithium-ion cells with a solid material. That swap promises higher energy density, faster charging and a lower risk of fire, which is why most major automakers are racing to bring them to market.

Prototype cells have shown ranges of more than 900 km in testing, though real-world figures will depend on pack design and vehicle weight. Charging from 10 to 80 percent in about ten minutes has been demonstrated in laboratory conditions.

Manufacturing is the main obstacle. Solid electrolytes are hard to produce at scale, and early cells have struggled with cracking over repeated charge cycles. Several companies now target limited production between 2027 and 2028.
