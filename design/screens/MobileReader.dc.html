Why Urban Trees Cool Cities in Summer

Oct 25, 2026 · 9 sources · 401 words

As cities continue to grow and urban populations increase, the need for effective cooling strategies becomes more pressing, especially during the sweltering summer months. One of the most effective and natural solutions lies in the presence of urban trees.

Urban trees provide cooling primarily through two key processes: shade and evapotranspiration. When trees shade buildings, sidewalks, and streets, they significantly reduce surface temperatures. This cooling effect can lower temperatures by 1-5°C, making a noticeable difference in the comfort levels of urban dwellers.

The impact of increasing tree cover in urban settings cannot be overstated. Research indicates that raising tree cover to 30% could prevent a significant number of heat-related deaths and reduce urban temperatures by an average of 0.4°C.
